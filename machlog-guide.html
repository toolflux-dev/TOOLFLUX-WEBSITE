Getting started

  1. Open the app. Go to toolflux-dev.github.io/machlog-app in Chrome on your phone or PC. Add it to the home screen, or bookmark it. On a shop PC without good internet you can download it as one file instead.
  2. Answer two questions. Your workshop's name and your name. Your 14-day free trial starts with every feature on. No card is needed.
  3. Set up your first job. The part number and the tool cutting it: its insert, what one insert costs and how many corners it has. If you have already made some pieces with it today, type them in. You can also skip this and look around first.

It works without internet. Once the page has opened, everything runs on your device. When there is internet, your entries are copied to the cloud by themselves.

Plan a job

A job is one part you are making for a customer, for example FLG-2210, a flange for Kirloskar, 500 pieces. You plan it once, when you take the order; the shop floor then logs against that plan.

  1. Jobs → + New job. Enter the part number, a description, the customer or PO, and the order quantity. The order quantity gives you progress and the likely finish date.
  2. The job opens on its Process plan. Add the operations in the order the part is machined: Op-1 turning, Op-2 on the VMC, and so on. Tap Rename to give each one a name the shop uses.
  3. Add the tools to each operation. For each tool: a name (OD turning, Ø12 drilling), its kind, the machine, the insert or tool (pick it from Inventory to fill the price), the cost of one insert, the corners per insert, and for a milling cutter how many inserts it holds.
  4. Tap "Plan done — go to production". Operators now see the job and its tools on the Production tab.

Planned is not loaded. A tool on the plan has nothing in the machine until someone records the first load. Tick "Already loaded in the machine" only if the inserts are in right now. Otherwise the operator records it in the log form the first time, as below.

Log production

This is the screen the operator uses every shift. On a phone, tap Log in the bottom bar and pick the job; on a PC, open the job and tap + Log production.

  1. Type the pieces made, or tap +5, +10, +25, +100. On an operator's own phone the name is already filled in; the machine is remembered from last time.
  2. Rejected pieces, if any. Type how many and tap the reason. A reason is required, so every rejected piece is explained.
  3. Tool changes, if any. For each tool you touched, tap Indexed, Replaced — worn or Replaced — broke. The first time a planned tool goes in, tap Loaded new inserts.
  4. Tap Save entry. The entry is saved at once and sent to the cloud.

A slipped digit is caught. If the count is far above that job's usual entry (500 typed for 50), the app asks before saving. Operators cannot edit an entry afterwards; the owner can.

Your shop's own rejection reasons go in Settings → Rejection reasons, one per line.

Insert life and corners

Tap any tool on a job to open it. The ring shows the insert corner by corner: used corners in dark, the corner in the cut filling up as it wears, and the corners still to come in pale teal. Below it, every set and every corner is listed with the pieces it made, against a mark for the usual life.

  • The usual life is learned from your shop: the average pieces per corner of the corners already used. The tool shows "about N left" and turns amber, then red when the corner is due.
  • An early failure is flagged the same day: a corner that broke at under 55% of the usual life. That usually means a wrong grade, a bad speed or feed, or a bad batch of inserts.
  • The owner can index or replace from the tool's page with "Index to the next corner" or "Replace the inserts". Operators record changes in the log form, so each change is tied to a count.

Jobs with several operations

Many parts need more than one setup. Each operation has its own tools, so a drill used in Op-2 never mixes its cost into Op-1. When a job has more than one operation, the job page shows them in a row with the good pieces and rejection rate of each, and the pieces waiting between them.

  • The log form names the operation being logged, and "Today so far" counts that operation.
  • A part is counted once: when it comes off the last operation. "Made today" and the job's total do not add Op-1 and Op-2 together.
  • Rejections from every operation are counted, because scrap on Op-1 is real scrap too.

Cost per piece

Cost per piece is the money spent on inserts divided by the pieces made with them. It comes from the sets you actually loaded and the pieces you actually logged. Only the owner sees it.

Worked example: OD Turning
Insert: CNMG 120408, 4 corners₹420 an insert
Set fully used, all 4 corners₹420 spent
Pieces made on that set352 pcs
Cost per piece₹420 ÷ 352 = ₹1.19
  • "So far" counts the corners started on the set in the machine: two corners started on a ₹420 insert counts ₹210.
  • "Finished sets" counts only sets that are used up. It is the figure to rely on.
  • For a whole job, the tooling of every operation is divided by the good pieces that came off the last operation.
  • Cost per piece in the sidebar ranks every insert you have run, cheapest per piece first. It is the fair way to compare a ₹280 insert with a ₹420 one.

The Overview

The owner's home screen. The line under the title says what happened today. Then:

  • The figures: made today against yesterday, made this week, rejected this week with the main reason, tooling spend for 7 and 30 days, and tooling per good piece.
  • Pieces made, last 7 days, with today in full teal and rejections in red.
  • On the machines: each running job with its order progress, the days left at the current pace, and its tools.
  • Operators this week, ranked by good pieces, with their percentage good.
  • Needs you: what to act on first, each one a tap from its fix. To buy and Tool watch sit beside it.

Inventory and buying

  • Add the inserts and tools you keep: designation, brand, grade, unit price, stock, and a reorder level. Or paste a table from Excel; columns named Product, Brand, Price and Qty are recognised.
  • Link a tool on the plan to an inventory item. Its price fills itself, and stock comes down every time inserts are loaded on it.
  • Tap Update on an item when stock arrives, the price changes, or a count was wrong.
  • To buy lists what is out, at its reorder level, or about to run out at the rate you use it. The suggested order covers about 30 days and rounds inserts up to boxes of 10. Send list on WhatsApp sends items and quantities to your supplier, without prices.

Team and the owner PIN

  1. Team → + Add operator. The first time, the app asks you to set an owner PIN of 4 to 8 digits. From then on costs, reports, the plan and edits stay behind it.
  2. Send the operator their link with Send or Copy link. They open it on their phone and it starts straight on the shop-floor screen, locked to operator use, with their name filled in.
  3. Their entries reach your device within about a minute when both phones have internet.

Shift proof

At the end of the shift the operator opens Shift proof and taps Send on WhatsApp. The owner sees who worked, what was made on which machine, the rejections and tool changes, and whether the entries already reached the cloud. "Copy" puts the same message on the clipboard; "Print / PDF" prints it.

Reports and export

  • Report: a black-and-white A4 report for any job, from the job's Report tab. Each tool's sets, usual life, pieces and cost per piece, with early failures marked and a line to sign.
  • Export to Excel: a workbook with four sheets: jobs, the production log, cost per piece by tool, and early failures.
  • Backup file: Settings → Backup JSON saves everything to one file for a pen drive; Restore JSON brings it back.

Backup and working offline

  • Every entry is saved on the device first, so the app works with no signal at all.
  • When there is internet, every entry, insert change, edit and delete is copied to your cloud sheet within seconds. Without it, changes wait and send themselves later, even after the browser restarts.
  • The line at the foot of the Overview says where you stand: Backed up, Backing up, or Offline.

Clearing the browser's site data deletes the copy on that device. The cloud sheet keeps everything that was sent. Take an Excel export or a backup file now and then.

Plans and billing

The first 14 days are free with every feature. After that, choose a plan by how many operator phones need to log: Solo ₹299 a month for one, Shop ₹799 for up to five, Works ₹2,499 for any number. Every plan has every feature.

Subscribe from Plan & billing and pay through Razorpay. On another device, tap Activate my subscription with the email you paid with. If a subscription ends, nothing is deleted: the app becomes read-only, so you can still see and export everything until you subscribe again.

Questions

I forgot my owner PIN.

Message TOOLFLUX on WhatsApp at 86602 34766 and we will walk you through a reset. Your data is safe.

An operator logged the wrong count.

Unlock with your PIN, open the job, find the entry in the production log and tap Edit.

Can two PCs or phones share one shop?

Yes. Your device is the shop; add each operator in Team and send them their link, and their entries come into your shop. A second device opened without a link starts a separate shop of its own.

A tool shows "nothing recorded as loaded".

Pieces are being logged on its operation, but nobody recorded putting inserts in. In the next entry, tap "Loaded new inserts" for that tool. Until then its corner life and cost are not counted.

How do I get updates?

They arrive by themselves. Close and reopen the app, or refresh the page, and you are on the latest version.
