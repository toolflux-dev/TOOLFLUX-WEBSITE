L/D Ratio: Why Deep Hole Drilling Fails — and How to Fix It

TOOLFLUX Engineering · June 2026 · 4 min read

What Is L/D Ratio?

L/D ratio is the hole depth (L) divided by the drill diameter (D). A 20mm deep hole drilled with a Ø10mm drill is a 2D hole. The same hole with a Ø5mm drill is 4D. Most machinists program the same feed and speed for a 2D and an 8D hole — then wonder why the deeper hole fails.

Why Depth Kills Performance

Beyond 3D, three things go wrong simultaneously: chip evacuation becomes harder, coolant reach decreases, and the drill body deflects more due to longer unsupported length. Each compounds the others.

A chip that cannot evacuate packs against the flutes, scores the hole wall (oversize hole, poor finish) and eventually causes catastrophic drill failure — the drill seizes and breaks. This is the most common failure mode for deep holes on production lines.

The Numbers That Actually Work

  • 2D holes: Full rated Vc and fn. No correction needed.
  • 3D holes: Vc × 0.90, fn × 0.90. Peck every 1×D on steel.
  • 5D holes: Vc × 0.75, fn × 0.80. Through-coolant strongly recommended. Peck every 0.5×D.
  • 8D holes: Vc × 0.60, fn × 0.65. Through-coolant mandatory (min 50 bar). Peck every 0.3×D. Reduce entry feed to 50% for first 5mm.

Solid Carbide vs U-Drill for Deep Holes

For holes above Ø14mm and deeper than 2D, a U-drill (indexable) almost always beats solid carbide on cost-per-hole. Insert replacement cost vs regrinding vs disposal of solid carbide makes U-drill economical at scale.

For Ø14mm and below, solid carbide with internal coolant is the only viable option for 5D+ drilling.

Entry Feed — The One Rule That Doubles Drill Life

Always reduce feed to 50% for the first 3mm of drill entry. The entry is where impact load is highest and edge fracture most likely. After the full cutting edge is engaged, ramp to full programmed feed. This single habit will double your drill life on interrupted surfaces and castings.
